Sensors are what make a smart home feel automatic: lights that turn on by themselves, alerts when something opens or leaks. Choose them for the job and place them well.

What to check

  • Door and window sensor: alerts you when something opens or closes, and can trigger scenes such as turning lights off when you leave.
  • PIR motion sensor vs mmWave presence sensor: the first picks up movement, while the radar type is offered to detect that a person is present even when fairly still, so it suits rooms where you sit without moving much. Mount it where a curtain or fan does not move constantly in front of it.
  • Temperature and humidity sensor: keep it away from direct sun and hot appliances, or it will report values that do not reflect the room.
  • Leak and soil sensors: put the leak sensor near a sink, water heater or washing machine, and put the soil sensor in the plant's root zone, not on the surface.
  • Smoke and gas: a useful extra, not a replacement for the approved alarms in your home. Test them regularly and replace batteries.

Common mistakes

  • Pointing a motion sensor at a window, heater or fan, which can cause false triggers.
  • Mounting a door sensor with the two parts too far apart, so it reports wrongly. Follow the gap given on the product page.
  • Forgetting the batteries: most sensors are battery powered, so set a reminder to check them.

Questions people ask

How long do the batteries last?

It varies by model and use, from months to a year or more. Treat advertised battery life as a rough guide.

Do sensors need a hub?

Zigbee sensors, and some Bluetooth ones, need a gateway. Wi-Fi sensors connect to your router directly but often use more power.

Can sensors work without internet?

Some systems run local automations on the gateway, while cloud-based scenes need internet. It depends on the app and gateway.
