A smart plug turns any device with a simple on/off switch into one you can schedule or control from your phone. It is easy, but the plug type and load rating matter.

What to check

  • Plug shape and voltage: Algeria commonly uses type C and F plugs at 230 V, while many US plugs are type A and B at 120 V. Do not buy a plug that does not match your mains.
  • Maximum load: check the amp or watt rating printed on the plug and never exceed it. Large appliances such as heaters and air conditioners need a plug explicitly rated for them.
  • Connectivity: many work on 2.4 GHz only.
  • Features: schedules, timers and energy monitoring are useful when available.

Common mistakes

  • Plugging a heater, kettle or air conditioner into a plug that is not rated for that load.
  • Buying the wrong plug type for your country and relying on a loose adapter, which is best avoided for high loads.
  • Assuming the device stays off after a power cut: the behaviour when power returns differs by model, so check the setting in the app.

Questions people ask

Which devices suit a smart plug?

Lamps, fans, chargers and similar simple devices. Avoid devices that should never switch on while nobody is there, such as irons and heaters, unless you accept the risk.

Does it work when the Wi-Fi is down?

On some models, schedules stored on the plug keep running, but app control usually needs your router. This differs by model, so check the listing.

Can it measure power use?

Only models that state energy monitoring. The figures are approximate, useful for comparing devices rather than for checking a bill.
