Zigbee and Wi-Fi are two ways devices talk to your phone. Neither is better for everyone: the right choice depends on how many devices you plan to add and what you already own.

What to check

  • Wi-Fi: needs no gateway, as the device connects straight to your router. But many devices work on 2.4 GHz only, and a lot of devices can burden a home router.
  • Zigbee: a wireless network made for smart home devices that needs a Zigbee gateway, sold separately. The gateway links the devices to your router and the app, and it suits battery-powered sensors and buttons.
  • Before buying: many products are sold in two versions (Wi-Fi and Zigbee) under the same name, so choose the version on the product page. If you pick Zigbee, make sure you have a gateway that supports the same app, such as Smart Life / Tuya.
  • To start: with only two or three devices, Wi-Fi is simpler. If you will add many sensors, a Zigbee gateway keeps things tidy.

Common mistakes

  • Buying Zigbee sensors without buying a gateway.
  • Buying a gateway that works with a different app from your devices.
  • Hiding the gateway in a closed cabinet or placing it far from the devices, then wondering why distant ones drop out.

Questions people ask

What is a Zigbee gateway?

A small hub that connects to your router and talks to Zigbee devices, so your phone can control them through an app.

Do Zigbee devices work with Alexa or Google Home?

Often, through the gateway's app or a compatible hub, depending on the gateway. Check the gateway's product page for the assistants it supports.

What about Matter?

Matter is a newer standard that aims to make devices from different brands work together. Not every device or gateway supports it, so check the listing instead of assuming.
