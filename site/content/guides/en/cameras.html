Cameras are the smart home device people most often regret buying wrongly: surprise subscriptions, weak night images, or a view they should not be recording. These points help you choose well.

What to check

  • Storage: a microSD card records locally, while cloud storage usually needs a subscription. Know the difference before buying.
  • Resolution and night vision: 1080p is enough for many uses and 2K shows more detail. Pan/tilt cameras cover more space.
  • Privacy and law: point the camera at your own property only, tell household members and guests, and check local recording laws.
  • Security: set a strong password, enable two-step verification if offered, and keep firmware updated.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a camera for outdoor use without checking for a stated protection rating, such as IP65, on the product page.
  • Counting on cloud recording without checking the subscription price and what the free option includes.
  • Leaving the default password, or sharing the camera login with people outside the household.

Questions people ask

Does a camera work without internet?

Many record to a microSD card without internet, but live viewing on your phone and notifications normally need a connection.

Wi-Fi or wired?

Wi-Fi is easier to install, while a camera with an Ethernet port is generally more stable, which matters for one you rely on. If a model has the port, the product page shows it.

How much storage do I need?

It depends on resolution and on whether the camera records continuously or only on motion. Motion-only recording makes a card last much longer; check the largest card size the camera supports.
