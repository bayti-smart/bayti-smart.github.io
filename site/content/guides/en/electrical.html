Anything wired into the walls or the electrical panel is a job for a qualified electrician. This guide explains which smart home products involve mains wiring and which you can safely plug in yourself.

What to check

  • In-wall switches and modules: wall switches, dimmers and curtain modules are wired in. Switch off the main breaker before working, and some switches need a neutral wire, so check before buying.
  • DIN rail energy meter: installed inside the electrical panel, ideally by a qualified electrician, especially at high loads.
  • Maximum load: never exceed the value printed on the device, and do not put a smart plug on a heater or air conditioner unless it is explicitly rated for that.
  • Wired network: if you have several cameras or fixed devices, an Ethernet switch connects them by cable and takes load off Wi-Fi.

Common mistakes

  • Working on a circuit without switching off the breaker and checking that it is really dead.
  • Buying an in-wall switch without checking for a neutral wire or which load types it supports.
  • Using a smart plug on a high-power heater or air conditioner.

Questions people ask

Which products can I install myself?

Plug-in devices such as smart plugs, bulbs, cameras and sensors need no wiring. Anything that replaces a switch or goes in the panel involves mains wiring.

What is a neutral wire and why does it matter?

Neutral is the return wire that many in-wall smart switches need to power themselves. Older homes sometimes lack it at the switch box, so ask an electrician or choose a model that says it works without one.

Do I need a permit or an inspection?

Rules differ by country and by the type of work, so ask a licensed electrician or your local authority instead of assuming.
