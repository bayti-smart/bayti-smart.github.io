Most smart-home problems are network problems. A few habits keep your devices connected and your home network safer.

What to check

  • 2.4 GHz: many low-cost Wi-Fi devices only work on 2.4 GHz. If your router shows 2.4 and 5 GHz under one name, setup can fail; connect the phone to 2.4 GHz while pairing.
  • Router position: place it centrally, away from metal and thick walls. Distance and walls matter more than headline speed.
  • Guest network: if your router offers one, put smart devices there to keep them apart from your computers and phones.
  • Passwords: change default passwords, use a strong Wi-Fi password, and keep the router firmware up to date.
  • No open ports: do not expose cameras or devices to the internet; use the maker's app or a secure remote-access option.

Common mistakes

  • Pairing a device while the phone is on a 5 GHz-only network.
  • Leaving the router in a cabinet or a far corner, then blaming the device.
  • Keeping the default password on a camera or hub.

Questions people ask

Why does my device not connect during setup?

Most often the phone is on a 5 GHz network, or the signal at the device is weak. Move closer to the router and try again.

Do I need a mesh Wi-Fi system?

Only if some rooms have weak signal after you reposition the router. For a small flat, one router is usually enough.

What is a guest network for?

It keeps devices you trust less, such as cheap cameras, on a separate network from your computers and phones.
