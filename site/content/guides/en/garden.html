Garden automation is mostly about water and weather: a timer, a sensor and a reliable signal. Outdoor conditions are harder on electronics than indoor ones, so plan for that.

What to check

  • Watering timer or water valve: opens and closes the water on a schedule or from the app. Check your hose or pipe connector size before buying, as the device may need extra adapters.
  • Signal outdoors: for a Wi-Fi device, make sure your router's signal reaches its location. For Zigbee, the gateway sits indoors and needs a reasonable range.
  • Protection: outdoor electronics need shelter from direct sun and rain unless an explicit rating such as IP65 is stated.
  • Solar lights: mount them where the sun reaches most of the day, because shade shortens the lighting time at night.
  • Soil sensor with watering: helps you learn what the plant really needs before you set a watering schedule.

Common mistakes

  • Mounting electronics in direct sun and rain without a stated protection rating.
  • Choosing a Wi-Fi device without checking that the router signal reaches the garden.
  • Keeping the same watering schedule all year instead of adjusting for season and rain.

Questions people ask

Is a watering timer worth it for a balcony?

For a few pots, a drip kit with a simple timer can be enough. Check the water source and which connector fits it.

Battery or mains power?

Battery models are easier to place, while mains-powered ones need a safe, outdoor-suitable supply. Check how often batteries need changing.

How do I avoid overwatering?

Start with short watering times, watch the plants for a week, and add a soil sensor if you want to see how moist the soil really is.
