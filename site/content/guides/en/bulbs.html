A smart bulb is the cheapest way to try smart lighting, but the wrong base, voltage or network type is the most common reason one does not work. Run through this short list before you order.

What to check

  • Voltage and base: check your mains voltage (120 or 230 V) and bulb base (E27, E14, B22 or E26). Do not fit a bulb designed for 120 V on 230 V mains.
  • Connectivity: Wi-Fi bulbs need no hub, but many work on 2.4 GHz networks only. With many bulbs, hub-based systems can be more stable.
  • Brightness: measured in lumens. Also check color temperature: warm for living areas, cool for work.
  • Compatibility: check support for the voice assistant you use and the quality of the app.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a bulb with the wrong base, or one built for a voltage that does not match your mains.
  • Assuming a Zigbee bulb works without a gateway, or that a Wi-Fi bulb will connect to a 5 GHz-only network.
  • Fitting a bulb in an enclosed fixture without checking that it is suitable, because heat can shorten its life.

Questions people ask

Do I need a hub for smart bulbs?

Wi-Fi bulbs connect straight to your router and need no hub. Zigbee bulbs need a Zigbee gateway, and Bluetooth bulbs usually have a short range.

Why can't I control the bulb after switching it off at the wall?

A smart bulb needs power to stay reachable, so a wall switch that cuts power makes it disappear from the app. Leave the switch on and control the bulb from the app or a smart switch instead.

Can I mix bulbs from different brands?

Often yes, if they use the same app or standard, such as Tuya / Smart Life, or Zigbee with a compatible gateway. Check compatibility on each product page.
